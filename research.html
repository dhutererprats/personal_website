Space domain awareness

Space-based space surveillance

An orbiting optical sensor must track moving targets while managing its own attitude, power, storage, and communications. I develop closed-loop tasking policies that couple these spacecraft constraints with the geometry and illumination of resident space objects.

This research progresses from LEO-to-LEO imaging to observations across orbital regimes, then to persistent catalog maintenance with revisits and changing target priorities. The studies use Basilisk and BSK-RL spacecraft simulation.

Responsive Autonomous Resident Space Object Imaging and Catalog Maintenance Using Reinforcement Learning Across Orbital Regimes

Daniel Huterer Prats and Hanspeter Schaub

Persistent, priority-aware catalog maintenance by a single LEO observer imaging targets in LEO, MEO, and GEO. The formulation supports repeated observations, online target-priority promotions, and variable-duration imaging and downlink actions. The study adapts a target-wise attention policy and evaluates training-catalog composition, acquisition–delivery rewards, catalog-size transfer, and response to changing priorities.

Autonomous Space-Based Imaging: Reinforcement Learning Scheduling with Imaging–Downlink Tradeoffs Across Orbital Regimes

Daniel Huterer Prats and Hanspeter Schaub

Resource-aware scheduling for a LEO spacecraft observing objects across LEO, MEO, and GEO. A tunable acquisition–delivery objective connects imaging with data return to the ground. The study examines transfer of LEO-trained policies to mixed-orbit catalogs without retraining, alongside illumination-dependent tasking during eclipse.

Reinforcement Learning for Space-to-Space Surveillance: Autonomous Scheduling for Resident Space Object Imaging

Daniel Huterer Prats, Hanspeter Schaub, and Chris Wheeler

LEO-to-LEO optical imaging formulated as a partially observable decision process, coupling dynamic target selection with onboard resource management. An actor–critic policy is trained in Basilisk and BSK-RL and compared with a heuristic scheduler under spacecraft, line-of-sight, and illumination constraints.

Rendezvous and proximity operations

Autonomous satellite inspection

Close-range inspection requires a spacecraft to maneuver around an object, observe illuminated surfaces, and avoid collision. In co-authored work with Mark Stephenson and Hanspeter Schaub, we study closed-loop maneuver selection with an optimization-based safety shield.

IWPSS
2025

Autonomous Satellite Inspection in Low Earth Orbit with Optimization-Based Safety Guarantees

Mark Stephenson, Daniel Huterer Prats, and Hanspeter Schaub

A semi-Markov inspection formulation combines learned impulsive-maneuver decisions with an optimization-based collision-avoidance shield. The policy responds to the current relative spacecraft state rather than following a fixed offline maneuver sequence. The shield's guarantees are defined within the adopted Clohessy–Wiltshire–Hill relative-motion model.
