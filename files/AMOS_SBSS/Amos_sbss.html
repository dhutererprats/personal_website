Reinforcement Learning for Space-to-Space Surveillance

Autonomous scheduling for resident space object (RSO) imaging in LEO — learned target selection, energy/storage management, and downlink timing using an actor-critic agent in the BSK-RL ecosystem.

“Unlike Earth observation, space-to-space imaging has targets arriving from every direction at high relative rates, under tight visibility and illumination windows.”

The work below shows an RL agent that learns to schedule images of RSOs while managing energy, reaction wheels, and storage. The agent exploits eclipse for downlink, preserves margins without hand-crafted rules, and delivers more useful imagery than a myopic heuristic — all in high-fidelity simulation (Basilisk + BSK-RL).

Formulation (POMDP)

Imaging and resource management are posed as a partially observable MDP. The spacecraft must pick between imaging, charging, desaturating, and downlinking while targets move fast and visibility/illumination change quickly.

Dynamics and signals

$$ s_{k+1} = f\!\big(s_k,\, u_k\big), \quad o_k = h(s_k,t_k),\quad a_k = \pi_\theta(o_k),\quad u_k = g(a_k) $$

State $s_k$ includes energy, storage, reaction-wheel rates, and pointing; observation $o_k$ is what the on-board estimator provides; action $a_k$ selects a task (image/charge/desat/downlink) with duration; $u_k$ is the executed control in Basilisk.

Objective

$$ J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \gamma^k\, R(s_k,a_k,s_{k+1}) \right] $$

Rewards encourage imaging valuable, illuminated, in-LOS targets while maintaining margins and pushing data when downlink is available.

RL approach

  • Agent: advantage actor–critic with compact MLPs for on-board viability.
  • Environment: BSK-RL ties the RL loop to Basilisk’s high-fidelity spacecraft sim.
  • Actions: choose among 10 “image target” options (LOS-valid only) plus charge, desat, and downlink.
  • Signals: battery fraction, storage usage, RW speeds, sun/eclipse flags, ground-station access, candidate target geometry/illumination.
  • Training: on-policy rollouts across randomized catalogs and orbital phasing to promote generalization.
The agent learns habits operators like to see: image during good lighting, exploit eclipse to downlink, and keep margins without micromanagement.

Selected results

  • Generalization: policies carry over to new RSO catalogs and phasing.
  • Resource-aware scheduling: frequent downlink during eclipse; energy and wheel margins maintained without hand-coded rules.
  • Timeliness: more useful images delivered sooner than a myopic “closest-target” heuristic under the same constraints.

Reproducibility & resources

Training follows a standard rollout–update loop: run the current policy in $n_{\text{env}}$ parallel environments for $n_b$ episodes of length $N$, collect on-policy trajectories, then perform $n_{\text{opt}}$ epochs of stochastic gradient updates on mini-batches from those data. Repeat until a training-step budget $T$ is reached (updates $\approx T/(n_{\text{env}} n_b N)$).

In practice, a strong student desktop handles this—think several parallel sims for a few hours. Exact settings per experiment are in Table 4 of the paper; the open-source code and environment docs are linked below.
